Know if an email is a scam before you click.

PhishGuard adds a Scan button to Gmail. One click reads the open message, checks the sender, links and wording, and gives it a 0 to 100 scam risk score with the reasons why. It also checks every link you hover over and blocks the dangerous ones.

Add to Chrome Try the checker

Free to use with your own Groq API key. Works in Gmail on Chrome.

Try it on anything suspicious

Paste a link or a whole message. An AI model reads it, and every link is checked against PhishGuard’s blocklist. Or switch to Clean link to strip tracking from a URL before you open or share it.

What you paste is sent to an AI model on Groq for analysis, so don’t paste anything private. Results are estimates, not guarantees.

Three steps, no copy and paste

  1. Open any email

    PhishGuard sits quietly at the bottom of Gmail until you need it.

  2. Press Scan

    The sender, subject, body, links and attachment names go to an AI model for review.

  3. Read the verdict

    You get a score, a plain-English summary, and the specific red flags it found.

Every link is checked as you hover

Move your pointer over a link in any email. PhishGuard checks it on your device and shows what it found. Dangerous links are switched off.

Known-bad list

Links to addresses on the bundled blocklist are blocked, including whole servers that host malware.

Redirects and shorteners

A link that hides another site inside it, or a shortener that hides its destination, is blocked.

Switched off on the page

Blocked links lose their address and show a clear label, so there is nothing to click.

Works without hovering

Clicking, tapping, middle-clicking or pressing Enter on a blocked link is stopped too.

What PhishGuard looks at

The extension collects hard evidence from the page, then the AI weighs it against the wording of the message.

Sender identity

Look-alike domains, free-mail addresses posing as companies, and display names that don’t match the address.

Links that lie

Link text that shows one site but opens another, URL shorteners, raw IP addresses and disguised characters.

Pressure tactics

Fake deadlines, account-closure threats and “act now” language that pushes you to skip thinking.

Requests for sensitive things

Passwords, one-time codes, gift cards, crypto and payment details asked for by email.

Risky attachments

Executables, scripts, archives and macro-enabled files that commonly carry malware.

Reassuring signs too

Real newsletters and receipts score low. A link alone never raises the score.

Only scans when you ask

Nothing is read in the background. PhishGuard looks at one email, only after you press the button.

What is sent

  • Subject, sender, recipients and date
  • The first 6,000 characters of the body
  • Up to 20 links and the attachment names

What stays with you

  • Your Groq API key, kept in the extension and never shown to Gmail
  • Every other email in your inbox
  • Link checks, which run entirely on your device
  • Link cleaning, which strips trackers in your browser
  • No PhishGuard account and no tracking

Scores are AI estimates, not guarantees. When in doubt, don’t click, and contact the sender through a channel you already trust.

Install in two minutes

  1. Download the extension and unzip it.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder.
  4. Create a key at console.groq.com/keys and put it in the .env file inside the folder: 
    GROQ_API_KEY=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
  5. Reload Gmail, open an email, and press Scan this email.
